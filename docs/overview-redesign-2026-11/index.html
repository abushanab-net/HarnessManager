Where Astra changed the plan

  • Every instrument says how it knows. Astra's strongest point: a quota bar, a moving bead and a run-out time each make a promise. All three directions now mark values as measured (solid, with the time they were read), estimated (≈, dashed, "at this pace") or not read yet (—). Nothing missing is drawn as zero.
  • Orbit is held to real hops. Its rings are the four steps a token actually takes (account, tool, router, provider), not an invented hierarchy, and every limit arc names its window.
  • No decorative motion. Blueprint dropped its sweeping scan line. What moves is traffic, a change you made, or a limit getting close.
  • Runway separates the two times. The reset is measured; the run-out is an estimate with a band around it. The headline says which is which.
  • Three different jobs. Blueprint helps you understand the setup, Orbit follows live activity, Runway plans the capacity you have left.

Why it looks different on each computer

What the current CSS does

  • Display scale changes the width the app gets, not just its sharpness. A 1920 screen at 150% gives WebView2 1280 pixels to lay out. The fixed widths (228 px sidebar, 420 px search pushed 120 px right, the 230 px limits label column, six fixed account columns, the route map's 160/128/150/160 px boxes) take a bigger share at 1280 than at 1920, so everything else squeezes.
  • Spacing is chosen per component. The sheet pads 22/24 px, cells 10/14 or 12/14, gaps 14, 16, 18, 20, 22 and 24. Nothing ties them together, so two pages never quite line up.
  • Eleven type sizes from 10.5 to 28 px, all fixed, including half pixels that render differently at 125%.
  • Five media queries look at the window, not at the sheet, so a page breaks at a different point depending on the sidebar and the scale.
  • The route map's canvas redraws for theme and width only, so moving the window between monitors with different scales leaves it soft.

The foundation all three directions share

  • One spacing scale in rem: 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48. Cells pad 16, groups sit 24 apart, the sheet pads 24 by 32. Nothing in between.
  • Five reading sizes, fixed: 12, 13, 14, 16, 20. Only the page title and the key numbers are fluid (clamp() against the sheet's width), so tables never shrink.
  • Container queries on the sheet: the page rearranges by the room it actually has (below 70 rem and 56 rem), whatever the scale, the screen or the sidebar. The sidebar folds to icons only under 75 rem of app width.
  • Groups are blocks of shared hairlines (from Pulser), so internal spacing cannot drift.
  • Graphics are measured, not positioned with calc(): the route maps lay out for the width they get and redraw when the scale changes.
Direction A · from KERNL and Pulser

Blueprint

Your setup as a technical drawing. Every token's route is drafted with right-angle lines and callouts, limits are dimension lines from now to the reset, and a rail says what needs you. Light is drafting paper with teal ink; dark is a cyanotype.

The one bold thingThe drafted route map: hover any box to trace every hop its tokens take; the hottest route, a paused provider and an expired login carry callouts.
Measured against estimatedLimits show what is used (solid gauge) and when it resets (solid dimension line), then the estimate at this pace (dashed tick, ≈, the red cross where it runs out).
MotionRoutes draw in once when the page opens, then square beads travel each line as fast as its traffic. Nothing else moves.

Direction B · from Orbit, CLUST and GPUWise

Orbit

Live activity as a scene. This computer sits at the centre; the rings are the real hops, accounts, then tools, then the router, with providers on the outside. Tokens fly along the hops they take, and each account wears its limit as an arc. Drag to turn it. Dark is the night sky; light is porcelain with ink lines.

The one bold thingThe orbital route map, drawn in WebGL behind HTML labels that stay sharp and readable. Hover a body to isolate its path; drag to rotate.
Measured against estimatedEach account's arc is the used share of its tightest window (solid) and its forecast at the reset (faint), with the window's name and reset under the label.
MotionParticles move in proportion to the last five minutes of traffic. A paused provider flickers red. Still mode draws one frame.

Direction C · from Vercel, Linear and Pulser

Runway

Capacity over time first. The headline is the answer, the hero is a seven-day runway with one lane per account, and you can drag "now" forward to see what happens at today's pace. Routes become a single flow strip; requests become a ledger. Calm, in both themes.

The one bold thingThe runway: drag the "now" handle into the week and every lane, the headline and the key numbers project forward, with an uncertainty band that widens the further you look.
Measured against estimatedLeft of now is measured; right of now is an estimate, drawn as a band, never as a hard line. Resets are measured and marked as flags.
MotionOnly the flow strip moves, and only as fast as its traffic. Dragging animates in answer to your hand.
