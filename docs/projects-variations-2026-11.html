Variation A · Cards, 12 a page

The current layout kept, with the eight fixes. Four cards a row, a pager underneath, the chart across the whole card, the pin and tag icons in the card head, the pencil on hover, and the whole card opens the project. Best when you recognise projects by their shape of activity.

A · Cardstry: hover a card, click the pin, double-click a name, click the card

Why it works

  • Closest to what you have; the fixes are surgical.
  • The sparkline is the biggest element: activity is the first thing you see.

Costs

  • Twelve projects a page: Bunyan and the rest need three pages at 37.
  • Cost and sessions are not in columns, so projects are harder to compare than in B.

Variation B · Table, 25 a page

One row per project with sortable columns: name, activity, sessions, tokens, cost, accounts, last active. Dense enough that 37 projects fit on two pages; the icons sit at the right of the row and show on hover; the row opens the project. Best when you compare projects by their numbers.

B · Tabletry: click a column header to sort, hover a row, click the pin, double-click a name, click the row

Why it works

  • Everything comparable in columns; sorting by any of them.
  • Most projects per page; the pager is rarely needed.

Costs

  • The chart is a small cell; less of a picture of each project.
  • Looks like Usage by account; less of its own page.

Variation C · Row cards, 10 a page

A wide card per project: name and path on the left, a full-width area chart of its activity in the middle, cost and tokens, then sessions, accounts and last active, and the icons at the right. The chart gets the most room of the three. Best when the trend of each project matters more than density.

C · Row cardstry: hover a row, click the pin, double-click a name, click the row

Why it works

  • A real chart per project, wide enough to read.
  • Numbers still line up down the page, so projects compare.

Costs

  • Ten a page: four pages at 37 projects.
  • Tall rows; the first screen shows about six projects.

D1 · Tabs on Accounts

Accounts gets a tab row: Accounts is today's page, Usage is today's Usage by account page. The smallest change; nothing on either tab moves.

D1 · Tabstry: the tabs, the 7 / 14 / 30 range on the Usage tab (redraws the panels in place)

Why it works

  • One entry fewer in the sidebar, nothing to relearn.
  • Both tabs keep their room: the ledger stays a full table.

Costs

  • Usage is still a separate screen, one click further than today.
  • The account rows still show only "tokens · 7 days": no cost beside the limits.

D2 · One page: usage in the rows, the chart above the groups

Each account row gains a sparkline, its cost and its requests for the range; each tool group header totals them. The chart of tokens per day folds above the groups; By model folds below. The range and Export CSV sit in the page header. One screen answers both "which account has room" and "what did each cost".

D2 · One pagetry: the range (rows, headers and chart redraw in place), fold the chart and By model

Why it works

  • Room and cost side by side, per account, where you choose which one to start.
  • No second screen; the chart is there when wanted and folds away.

Costs

  • Wider rows: input, output and cache read move into a tooltip on the tokens, not columns.
  • A longer page when every tool has several accounts.

D3 · List on the left, usage on the right

A compact account list on the left (name, plan, the tightest limit as a bar, 7-day tokens), grouped by tool. The right pane shows All accounts (the stacked chart, the ledger, By model) or, when an account is chosen, that account's own chart, its models and its numbers, with Start, Log in and the ⋯ menu in the pane's header. Clicking the name opens the account page as today.

D3 · List and panetry: pick an account on the left, then All accounts

Why it works

  • Usage per account at one click without leaving the page; the whole-fleet view one click back.
  • Scales to many accounts: the list is compact.

Costs

  • The limits lose their room: one bar per account instead of every tier.
  • The biggest change of the three; the first-run tour and the tests follow.
Recommendation for this merge: D2. Accounts is the page you open to pick where to work next, and cost beside room is the number that decides it. D1 is the fallback if the wider rows feel crowded on your screens; D3 only if the account count grows past ten.
